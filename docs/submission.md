# NASA Space Apps 2026 — submission texts (ready to paste)

## Project name
AralSentinel

## High-level summary (≤ 200 words)
AralSentinel helps farmers in Uzbekistan's water-scarce, salinity-prone irrigated lands choose crop rotations with NASA data. Jizzakh, our pilot region, received only 342 mm of rain a year over the last five years and almost none in summer (about 16 mm from June to August), while MODIS measured summer land-surface temperatures of about 40 °C. For any field, the web app combines ten years (2015–2025) of NASA POWER (MERRA-2) rainfall, root-zone soil wetness and temperature with MODIS Terra NDVI and land-surface temperature, and with ISRIC SoilGrids soil properties. From these it produces a transparent salinity/drought proxy risk index. Using FAO rules (FAO-33, FAO-56, FAO-29), it generates and ranks three-year crop rotations by the farmer's own priorities: saving water, protecting soil or income. It also warns about the "alfalfa trap" on water-scarce fields. A water-cut simulator shows the yield and income lost if irrigation is cut by 10–50 %. The app is free, runs on a phone in English, Uzbek and Russian, works offline, and states its limitations openly: the risk score is a proxy and the prices are estimates.

## Project details

**What it does**
- Map of demo fields (Jizzakh, Syrdarya, Tashkent, Karakalpakstan). Clicking a field shows:
  - **10-year trends:** MODIS NDVI, MODIS summer land-surface temperature, NASA POWER root-zone soil wetness, rainfall and summer peak air temperature.
  - **A 0–100 salinity/drought proxy risk index** with its main driver and any missing inputs. Example: Jizzakh field 23 (low); Karakalpakstan field 40 (medium).
  - **The top 5 three-year rotations** (Year 1 → Year 2 → Year 3) with fit, water-saving, salt-tolerance, soil-health and estimated income scores, re-ranked live by the priority sliders.
  - **A water-cut simulator.** Example: for the Jizzakh field's best rotation, a −20 % water cut gives −20.3 % yield and −$214/ha/yr (estimate).
- Interface in English, Uzbek and Russian.

**How it works**
1. `backend/fetch_data.py` downloads and caches the following once (with retries and backoff), then builds `data/field_data.json`:
   - NASA POWER monthly data, 2015–2025
   - MODIS MOD13Q1 and MOD11A2 subsets from the ORNL DAAC (Jun–Aug of each year)
   - SoilGrids properties at 0–30 cm
2. **Risk proxy:** weighted, re-normalised components:
   - low rainfall (0.20)
   - dry root zone (0.20)
   - summer heat (0.20)
   - NDVI trend (0.25)
   - clay and pH (0.15)
3. **Rotations:** every 3-year sequence of 6 crops (cotton, winter wheat, alfalfa, maize, sorghum, mung bean) is generated and filtered by these rules:
   - no repeat in consecutive years
   - at least 1 legume
   - alfalfa only where water availability is high
   - at least 2 salt-tolerant years on high-risk fields

   Each rotation is scored with FAO-33/56 water needs, FAO-29 salt tolerance, a soil-health rule of thumb and estimated income, then weighted by the user's priorities and by the field's risk.
4. **Water cut:** FAO-33, 1 − Ya/Ym = Ky × (1 − ETa/ETm).
5. **Serving:** a FastAPI backend serves the API and the frontend. `backend/export_static.py` exports all results as static JSON, so the public site is a static Netlify deployment that also works offline.

**Benefits**
- Free and open: no login, works on a phone, works without internet.
- Makes trade-offs between water, soil and income visible for one specific field.
- Quantifies the cost of a water cut before the season.
- Prevents water-hungry recommendations on water-scarce fields (the "alfalfa trap").
- Transparent: sources, assumptions and limitations are shown in the app.

**Tools and languages**
- Python 3 (FastAPI, Uvicorn, Requests)
- JavaScript, HTML, CSS, Leaflet 1.9.4, Chart.js 4.4.1
- Netlify for static hosting, GitHub for code
- Optional: Render for the live API

## NASA data used
- **NASA POWER** monthly point API, Agroclimatology community (MERRA-2 based): PRECTOTCORR (precipitation), GWETROOT (root-zone soil wetness), T2M_MAX (maximum air temperature), 2015–2025. https://power.larc.nasa.gov/
- **MODIS Terra MOD13Q1 v061**, 250 m 16-day NDVI (Jun–Aug mean per year). https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/products/mod13q1v061/ (doi:10.5067/MODIS/MOD13Q1.061)
- **MODIS Terra MOD11A2 v061**, 1 km 8-day land-surface temperature, LST_Day_1km (Jun–Aug mean per year). https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/products/mod11a2v061/ (doi:10.5067/MODIS/MOD11A2.061)
- MODIS subsets were accessed through the **ORNL DAAC MODIS Web Service**: https://modis.ornl.gov/data/modis_webservice.html

## Space agency partner data / other data
- **ISRIC SoilGrids v2.0**: soil pH, clay, sand and organic carbon, 0–30 cm. https://soilgrids.org/
- **FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 33**, Yield response to water (Doorenbos & Kassam, 1979): Ky factors and the yield-response equation.
- **FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 56**, Crop evapotranspiration (Allen et al., 1998): crop water needs.
- **FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 29 Rev.1**, Water quality for agriculture (Ayers & Westcot, 1985): crop salt-tolerance thresholds.
- Basemap: Esri World Imagery, with an OpenStreetMap fallback.
