# AralSentinel — 2-minute demo video script

- Screen recording plus voice-over only; no people on camera.
- Open https://symphonious-twilight-34e8d7.netlify.app in a fresh browser window at 1920 × 1080, language English, default sliders (water 0.4 / soil 0.3 / income 0.3).
- All numbers below are what the live app shows. Prices and income are estimates; say "estimate" when you read them.

| Time | Section | On screen (what to click) | Voice-over |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | **Hook** | Site opens on the satellite map; the Jizzakh report loads by itself. Slowly hover over the Jizzakh marker. | "This field in Jizzakh, Uzbekistan, got 342 millimetres of rain a year over the last five years, and only about 16 millimetres all summer. What should a farmer plant here next year?" |
| 0:15–0:35 | **Problem** | Scroll to the rainfall chart, then the land-surface temperature chart. | "Uzbekistan's irrigated land carries the legacy of the Aral Sea crisis: decades of cotton and wheat, heavy irrigation, and salt left behind in the soil. Water allocations are shrinking, and farmers rarely get field-level evidence of which fields are at risk." |
| 0:35–1:20 | **Live demo** | 0:35 point at the risk card (**23 · Low risk · proxy index**, "Main driver: declining vegetation (NDVI)"). 0:45 scroll to the rotation table: top row **Winter wheat → Sorghum → Mung bean, fit 69**. 0:55 drag *Save water* to 0 and *Income* to max: the top row becomes **Cotton → Winter wheat → Alfalfa, fit 81**. Drag back to defaults. 1:05 in the water-cut simulator click **−20 %** (−20.3 % yield, about −$214/ha/yr), then **−50 %** (−50.8 %, about −$535). 1:12 click **Karakalpakstan** in the field list: risk **40 · Medium**, alfalfa-trap warning appears. 1:17 switch the language to **O'zbekcha**, then **Русский**, then back to English. | "AralSentinel turns ten years of NASA data into one page per field. This is a transparent risk index — here low, driven by declining vegetation. Below it, the best three-year rotations for this field. If the farmer cares most about income, the ranking changes live, but notice the water-saving score falls. The water-cut simulator shows the price of a 20 percent cut: about 20 percent of the yield, around 214 dollars per hectare, an estimate. In dry Karakalpakstan the risk is medium, and the app warns against the alfalfa trap: a water-hungry crop on a water-scarce field. Everything works in Uzbek and Russian too." |
| 1:20–1:45 | **Science** | Scroll to the 5 charts, then open the **Limitations & honesty** section in the footer, then hover over the data-source links. | "The data: NASA POWER rainfall, root-zone soil wetness and temperature from MERRA-2; MODIS Terra NDVI and land-surface temperature through the ORNL DAAC; and SoilGrids soil texture and pH. Studies in Syrdarya show that MODIS canopy temperature tracks soil salinity, so heat is part of the index. Rotations follow FAO rules for water need, salt tolerance and yield response to water. It is a proxy, not a salinity sensor, and we say so on the page." |
| 1:45–2:00 | **Impact** | Point at the "Static version" banner (no server needed), click Tashkent: the report loads. End card: **AralSentinel** · https://symphonious-twilight-34e8d7.netlify.app · github.com/M10-MM/aralsentinel · "NASA Space Apps 2026 · Field Shift". | "It is free, open source, runs on a phone, needs no server, and a downloaded copy works fully offline, in three languages. AralSentinel helps farmers and extension agents decide before the season, not after the loss. Try it at the link on screen." |

Tips: rehearse once with a timer; hide the cursor between clicks; keep each number on screen for at least 2 seconds. Do not switch Wi-Fi off on the Netlify site: field reports are fetched on click. "Offline" means a local copy (the `frontend/` folder, or `backend\start.bat`).
